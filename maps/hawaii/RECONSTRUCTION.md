# Reconstruction and deliberate generalizations

## Grid and geography

Both maps use 84 × 54 pointy hexes, odd-row offsets, 9 km center spacing, and a Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection centered on 20.6° N, 157.5° W. Each hex represents approximately 70.15 km². This crop focuses on the eight main islands; the distant Northwestern Hawaiian Islands are outside it.

Modern land uses Natural Earth 1:10m polygons, with a 40% hex-area threshold. All eight main islands survive without minimum-island overrides. Kahoʻolawe is one modern hex. This coarse rasterization gives 252 land hexes, roughly 17,678 km², modestly above the source polygon area. Tiny islets and narrow bays are below resolution.

Elevation and bathymetry come from Mapzen/Tilezen Terrarium zoom-9 tiles. Thirty-seven samples per hex preserve central elevation, local relief, overall relief and seafloor distribution. These are mixed-source modern measurements; detailed marine saddle depths are less certain than the precision of the displayed integer suggests. Modern volcano shapes are held fixed: this is not a reconstruction of every lava flow, landslide, erosion event, tectonic movement or island subsidence since 21 ka.

## Modern terrain and vegetation

Broad shield slopes are passable hills; named summits and major eroded ridges use mountains. Eleven mountain landmarks include Mauna Kea, Mauna Loa, Kīlauea, Hualālai, Kohala, Haleakalā, West Maui, Waiʻaleʻale, the Koʻolau and Waiʻanae ranges, and East Molokaʻi. Four native volcano features mark Mauna Loa, Kīlauea, Hualālai and Haleakalā; this selection is a gameplay interpretation of volcanic activity, not an eruption forecast.

The University of Hawaiʻi Rainfall Atlas supplies 1978–2007 mean annual rainfall at 250 m resolution. Each hex uses the median of valid rainfall samples. Nearby measurements fill coastal gaps. Niʻihau is outside the atlas grid, so its dry climate uses an explicitly inferred 500 mm/year baseline. Terrain distinguishes dry leeward areas, moist windward slopes, the Kona moisture belt, and cold high-elevation volcanic ground.

Vegetation is **generalized potential climate cover**, not a contemporary forest-cover or land-use census. The model does not clear every pasture, plantation, town or recent lava flow. A simple 25.5°C sea-level baseline and 6°C/km lapse rate classify thermal belts. Broadleaf Forest represents mesic/montane woodland; Rainforest represents warmer, wetter canopy. Dry Hawaiian grassland is normally Plains, not a fabricated sandy desert. Desert at cold summits represents barren volcanic substrate. Forest and rainforest thresholds and hill thresholds are visible in the generator.

## LGM scenario (~21 ka)

The scenario lowers the sea by 125 m. Land expands when at least 40% of the 37 elevation samples lie above that level, retaining modern land. It adds 72 land hexes. The bathymetric sampling naturally connects Maui, Lānaʻi and Molokaʻi and exposes Penguin Bank; no forced land-bridge edits were needed. Kahoʻolawe and Niʻihau remain separate, consistent with the intended late-Pleistocene configuration. This is a sea-level approximation, not a time-dependent tectonic reconstruction.

Temperature is reduced by 4.5°C at the sea-level baseline; the 125 m increase in relative altitude contributes another 0.75°C under the chosen lapse rate. Vegetation belts therefore move downslope. Lowland/moisture classification uses **80% of reference rainfall** as a scenario assumption, informed by evidence for generally drier glacial conditions at some lowland sites. It is not a measured statewide paleo-rainfall raster or a claim that all islands experienced exactly the same anomaly. Cooler wet areas often change from Rainforest to Forest rather than becoming treeless.

Mauna Kea receives one impassable Snow Mountain hex, approximately the 70 km² area reported for its late-Pleistocene ice cap. Presence of ice is supported by USGS evidence; the hex boundary is an approximation, not a rasterization of a glacial-limit polygon. Ice is imposed from that evidence, not generated by the lowland rainfall multiplier. Enhanced high-elevation storm snowfall can coexist with drier lowlands. The present-day rainfall value shown in the inspector is reference data, not the ice cap's inferred snowfall.

Mauna Loa's possible glaciation is not marked as confirmed; later lava may have buried evidence. Haleakalā is not assigned an LGM glacier. There is no continental ice sheet or sea ice at Hawaiʻi. No large pluvial lakes are invented. Small crater lakes and ponds, including Waiau, are below a 70 km² hex's scale.

## Rivers and water travel

Named perennial USGS NHD streams are merged with relevant artificial flow paths through water features, avoiding gaps caused by omitting those records. A selected set of larger courses is routed onto exact native hex edges. Each placed course is contiguous, acyclic and ends at a coast vertex. Different watersheds avoid shared vertices. Small streams remain available in the source overlay; not every mapped stream receives a game river.

LGM courses are re-routed to the shifted coast; extensions across newly exposed shelves are inferred from modern drainage. The generator does not simulate hydraulic flow, groundwater, discharge changes or full paleo-catchments. Some river segments must shift by a hex, and only a few low, flat alluvial tiles receive floodplains. Modern has 27 river edges across 11 courses; LGM has 44 across 12.

**At the user's request, every island is reachable through Coast tiles.** A water-only shortest-path procedure joins the otherwise separate coastal networks. It converts 15 additional Ocean hexes to Coast in the modern map and 11 in the LGM map, without moving land or filling channels. Some are physically deep-water channels, deliberately classified as shallow navigable water for early Civ ships. Their original depths and explicit `game_coast_corridor` flags remain in the data. Reef placement remains tied to the depth-derived nearshore belt; the navigation corridors do not acquire artificial reefs or resources.

## Resources and suggested starts

Native resource placement rules constrain choices. Fish, crabs, whales and pearls support maritime play. Introduced crops and livestock are game resources in both versions; their LGM presence does **not** assert prehistoric Hawaiian agriculture, domestic animals, coffee cultivation or human settlement.

Kona has a coffee resource at its actual crop belt. Native Civ coffee requires flat Grass or Rainforest, so one existing Kona broadleaf-forest tile uses a rainforest canopy proxy without flattening its slope; the detail panel identifies this exception. Central Maui sugar uses a legal floodplain tile. Rice at Hanalei is a wet-agriculture/taro proxy. Hāmākua sugar cannot be placed faithfully on a sloping forest tile under the shipped rules and is not forced by inventing a marsh. Salt is a coastal-production abstraction. Iron and aluminum placements are balance concessions, not mapped commercial deposits; oil, coal and uranium remain absent.

Starts are selected from geographic island groups, not modern city coordinates: one each on Kauaʻi, Oʻahu and Maui, plus three on Hawaiʻi. Candidates require safe passable land, crop-edge clearance, river or coastal access, and at least six center-spacing units of separation. Scoring considers workable land, food and production. Food and luxury access are improved; the resource audit separately makes small, explicit pasture/plantation/quarry adjustments described below. The inspector reports actual freshwater status and integer native-hex separation. This does not make radically unequal islands equivalent, and it is not an in-engine yield simulation.

## Resource-audit revision

The user explicitly requested modern introduced resources in both climates. Thirteen missing resource types were added at 28 regional locations per version; see RESOURCE_AUDIT.md and resource_audit.json for evidence and per-tile changes. This adds localized land-use exceptions to the potential-vegetation model, including four cattle pastures, plantation/quarry clearing, cacao/vineyard canopy proxies and one Lānaʻi woodland-edge tile. No mountain or water topology is changed. Final resource counts are 242 modern and 270 LGM; the inspector reports 29 present types and 15 intentional zeroes.
